# Notes on the official samples (and what to avoid in third-party material)

Source files are in [materials/](materials/):
- **Official NAATI Vietnamese samples:** `Vietnamese - Education.pdf`, `Vietnamese - Employment.pdf`, `Vietnamese - Financial.pdf` and `Vietnamese - Social Services.pdf`, plus the matching `Vietnamese_*.mp3` audio.
- **Third-party practice dialogues:** `10.-Education-…pdf`, `11.-Health-Arthritis…pdf` and `14.-Legal-…pdf`.

## 1. What the 4 official dialogues look like

| Domain | English speaker | Vietnamese speaker | Situation | Segments | Words (EN + VI) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Education | School principal (F) | Mother (F) | Enrolling a newly arrived daughter: health, English test, documents | 12 | 152 + 149 = 301 |
| Employment | Job search case manager (M) | Job seeker (M) | Mutual obligations, former IT career, résumé, next appointment | 15 | 160 + 149 = 309 |
| Financial | Bank loan manager (M) | Client (F) | Buying a 2-bedroom apartment: price, deposit, savings, borrowing | 16 | 149 + 159 = 308 |
| Social Services | Centrelink social worker (F) | Mother (F) | Son applying for Youth Allowance after a family argument | 12 | 148 + 157 = 305 |

### Patterns to expect
- **Opening:** the professional greets you and introduces themselves by name ("Hello, I'm Jack. Thank you for coming in.").
- **Middle:** the Vietnamese speaker explains their situation **with feelings and family details** (worried, alone with 3 kids, afraid the child will fall behind). The professional asks questions and gives requirements, often as a **list**: *a utility bill, birth certificate, immunisation records, parents' details, visa status and passport copies*. Lists are where marks are lost, so note every item.
- **Numbers are few but critical:** 10 jobs a month, 10% deposit, $600,000, $70,000, 3.30, twice a week, 2 other children.
- **Ending:** arrangements and pleasantries, often light-hearted: a new shirt for the interview, kids "always starving" after school.
- **Very short segments do appear:** "How does 3.30 sound?" (4 words), *Vâng, thế thì tuyệt quá.* (2 words). Don't over-interpret them; keep them short.
- **Tone:** these are everyday conversations, not legal lectures. Expect fewer technical terms than in Dialogues 1–2 of the topic files, but natural, idiomatic speech.

### Dialect and register
- The Vietnamese **mixes Northern and Southern / diaspora forms**, sometimes in the same dialogue:
  - Northern: *Vâng, thế thì, à, ạ*.
  - Southern / diaspora: *chích ngừa, Bộ … hay sao, đô, ngày mốt, điện toán, ghi danh*.
  - Be ready to understand both. When you speak, pick one natural register and stay consistent.
- **How the client addresses the professional:** *ông / bà (+ surname)*, *thưa ông / bà*. Use the same when you interpret the professional's English into Vietnamese. Never use *bạn*.
- **Greetings are rendered by meaning, not literally:** *Xin chào ông* → "Good morning, Jack"; *Tôi hân hạnh được gặp ông* → "Nice to meet you".

## 3. Third-party samples: errors you should NOT learn

The three numbered PDFs (`10.-Education…`, `11.-Health-Arthritis…`, `14.-Legal…`) are useful for topic ideas, but their model answers contain mistakes that would lose marks. Use the corrections below.

### Format problems (not exam-realistic)
- Several segments are **38–47 words**. Real segments are ≤35.
- **Register:** they use *bạn* throughout (*Bạn có gọi cho cảnh sát…*, *Xin chào, bạn khỏe không?*). In a CCL dialogue, use *ông / bà / anh / chị*. *Thưa ngài* is too formal; *Chào ông* is natural.

### Vocabulary errors
These come from their vocab lists and model answers.

| Their rendering | Problem | Use instead |
|---|---|---|
| tenderness = *yếu ớt* | means "weak" | *đau khi ấn / đau khi chạm* |
| (at the) scene = *cảnh* | means "scenery" | *(tại) hiện trường* |
| copy (police report copy) = *sao chép* | that's the verb "to copy" | *bản sao* |
| looking forward = *chuyển tiếp* | means "forward (an email)" | *mong (được làm việc với ông/bà)* |
| deal (with) = *thoả thuận* | means "a deal / agreement" | *đối phó / vượt qua / đương đầu với* |
| notice = *thông báo* (in "I have noticed") | that's "a notice" | *nhận thấy* |
| bright (kid) = *ngoan* | *ngoan* = well-behaved | *sáng dạ / lanh lợi / thông minh* |
| Solicitor = *Luật* | means "law" | *luật sư* |
| Marriage breakdown = *kết hôn / tan vỡ* | *kết hôn* = get married | *hôn nhân đổ vỡ* |
| (medical) test = *bài kiểm tra* | that's a school test | *xét nghiệm* |
| statement = *tuyên bố* | that's a public declaration | *lời khai / lời nói* |
| He isn't social anymore = *không còn quảng giao* | rare, unnatural | *không còn hòa đồng / giao du với bạn bè như trước* |
| the counsellor … *anh ta* | slightly disrespectful | *anh ấy / thầy ấy* |

### English errors in their model answers
- "the joints of my hands are paining for a long time" → **"the joints in my hands have been hurting for a long time"**
- "still the pain doesn't subside" → **"but the pain still hasn't eased"**
- "I will don't do physical work" → **"I won't do any physical work"**
- "Thank you for meeting me" with a stated word count of 3 → shows their counts are unreliable.

### Content errors (don't learn as facts)
- **Arthritis:** they say you can prevent it by avoiding certain foods, and that red meat and legumes should be avoided completely. That's not medically sound. In the exam you interpret whatever is said, but don't take it as medical knowledge.
- **The "đèn xanh" sentence:** *Tôi đang đợi ở đèn xanh* ("waiting at a green light") is odd; it probably meant a red light. **In the exam, interpret what is actually said.** Correcting the speaker counts as a distortion.

## 4. Booklet facts worth remembering

These come from `CCL_Booklet_v_9_PP.pdf`. It is a third-party booklet; check against NAATI.
- Pass mark: **63/90 overall and at least 29/45 in each dialogue**. Example: 23 + 41 = 64 is still a **clear fail**.
- First repeat per dialogue is free; more can be penalised.
- To fix an error: "Sorry, I'll just say that part again", then correct it once.
- Results take about 4–6 weeks. The booklet says results are valid for 3 years and the fee is AUD 800, but both may be outdated, so check NAATI and Home Affairs.
- Test is ~20 min, plus 15 min to read instructions. Online, on a computer or laptop (no tablet or phone). Pen and blank paper allowed.
